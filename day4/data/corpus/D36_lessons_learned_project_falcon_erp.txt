PROJECT FALCON - LESSONS LEARNED (closure workshop held 24 Apr 2024)
Client: major European retail group | Duration: 14 months | Team: 38 FTE peak | Engagement lead: R. Albrecht

CONTEXT
Migration of 11 country entities from three legacy ERPs to a single cloud ERP, with go-live in two waves. Wave 1 (4 entities) went live in Oct 2023, wave 2 (7 entities) in Mar 2024. Budget variance at close: +8%. Schedule variance: wave 2 slipped 5 weeks.

WHAT WENT WELL
1. Data migration rehearsals. We ran four full mock loads before cutover. Each found new defects (duplicate suppliers, unit-of-measure mismatches) and by mock 4 the reconciliation difference was under 0.02%. Clients later said that this is what gave the CFO the confidence to sign off.
2. Joint war-room with client super-users during hypercare, with a shared defect triage board.
3. Early involvement of the client's internal audit in controls design.

WHAT DID NOT GO WELL
1. Late discovery of legal-entity-specific tax rules. Italian e-invoicing and Polish split-payment requirements were found in the build phase rather than in design. Cost: ~3 weeks rework.
2. Master-data ownership was unclear. For 5 months nobody on the client side owned "customer hierarchy", so decisions waited for steering committees.
3. Cutover plan was a spreadsheet owned by one person. During the wave 1 weekend the plan's dependencies were wrong in two places and tasks were run out of order; recovery took 9 hours.
4. We under-estimated training. Super-users were trained 3 weeks before go-live and forgot half of it; the help desk saw 3x the expected ticket volume for the first fortnight.
5. Change requests entered through three different channels (email, Jira, steering minutes), so scope creep was invisible until the financial review.

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR NEXT TIME
- Run a "local compliance sweep" per country in the discovery phase, signed off by local finance
- Name a data owner per master-data domain before design starts - a precondition in the SOW
- Use a rehearsable, version-controlled cutover runbook; do a dress rehearsal with timings
- Train closer to go-live but with a sandbox that super-users can keep using; plan refresher sessions
- Single change-control register with weekly review; any change > 5 person-days goes to the engagement partner
- Plan hypercare capacity at 2x expected volume for the first two weeks
- Keep a go/no-go checklist with objective thresholds (defect counts, reconciliation tolerance, training completion %)

QUOTES FROM THE CLIENT
"The rehearsals saved us." "We underestimated how many decisions we owed you."

KEY METRICS
Defects open at go-live: wave 1 - 17 (none Sev-1), wave 2 - 9 (none Sev-1). User satisfaction (post-hypercare): 7.4 / 10. Benefits tracking handed to the client PMO.
