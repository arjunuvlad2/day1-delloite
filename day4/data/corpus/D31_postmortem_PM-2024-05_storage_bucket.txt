PM-2024-05  Misconfigured storage container
Severity: SEV-1 (potential personal-data exposure)  Lead: CISO office

Summary: A project team created a blob container to share survey exports with a public-sector client's subcontractor. A default 'anonymous read' access level was left enabled. The container held 3,100 survey responses including free-text comments and email addresses. It was reachable from the internet for 11 days (3 - 14 May 2024) until a cloud-posture scanner raised a High finding and the team lead closed it.

Evidence of access: storage logs show 1,904 requests; 1,870 from known Northwind or client IPs, 34 from unknown addresses (mostly crawlers/security researchers). Cannot rule out download of the files.

Notifications: Client DPO informed within 24h of confirmation. The client, as data controller, decided with Legal that regulator notification within 72 hours was required, and it was made. Affected data subjects were notified.

Root causes
1. Self-service cloud subscription with permissive defaults
2. No mandatory review before sharing data externally
3. Posture-scanner findings were triaged weekly rather than daily for High severity
4. Team used survey exports with direct identifiers where pseudonymised data would have done

Remediations
- Policy-as-code (Azure Policy / SCP) to deny public access to storage accounts firm-wide - complete 30 May
- Secure transfer service (expiring links, recipient-limited) as the only approved sharing path
- High findings triaged within 4 hours
- Data minimisation checklist for projects handling personal data

Lesson: the most expensive control is the one that relies on people remembering a setting. Make the safe path the default path.
