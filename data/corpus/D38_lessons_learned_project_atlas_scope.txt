Atlas - Lessons on scope and acceptance (Dec 2023)

The contract was fixed-price with a 6-page functional description. The client treated every page of the "illustrative" mock-ups as committed scope. Over 7 months we absorbed ~420 person-days of unrecorded additions.

What we'd change:
1. Put acceptance criteria in the SOW per deliverable (measurable, testable). "User-friendly" is not a criterion.
2. Run a joint backlog refinement every sprint where the client product owner signs the sprint scope. The product owner must be empowered - ours was not and every decision escalated to a committee of nine.
3. A visible change-request log; give the client a price for each change within 5 working days.
4. Treat accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA) as an explicit line item; public-sector buyers require it and we under-priced it.
5. Do not demo features that are not in the plan "just to show willing". They become expectations.

Commercial outcome: margin on the engagement fell from planned 28% to 9%. Partner-level conversation with the client yielded a goodwill change budget for phase 2.

Take-away: scope is a contract topic first and a delivery topic second.
