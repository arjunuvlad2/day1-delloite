POL-HR-031 - Working from another jurisdiction (v1.2)
Owner: Global Mobility & Tax | Approver: General Counsel

SUMMARY
Colleagues sometimes ask to perform their duties while physically located outside the country in which they are employed and tax-resident. Doing so, even informally, can trigger personal income-tax, social-security and payroll-withholding obligations for the individual AND can create a permanent establishment (PE) or corporate-tax presence for Northwind in the host country. Local rules differ; the safest assumption is that foreign presence is NOT neutral.

WHAT IS ALLOWED WITHOUT PRE-APPROVAL
Nothing. Every overseas working arrangement must be registered via the Mobility Request form BEFORE travel. Pure leave (no laptop, no client work) is, of course, unaffected by this policy.

THRESHOLDS WE MONITOR
- Up to 20 working days per calendar year, cumulative, in a single treaty country: normally approvable by your Practice Lead + Global Mobility, subject to a tax check.
- 21-60 working days: needs Tax sign-off and may require a local payroll shadow.
- Over 60 working days, or any role that concludes contracts / manages local staff: treated as a relocation. Separate assignment letter.
- Countries on the restricted list (sanctions, no social-security agreement): refused.

WHAT YOU MUST NOT DO
 * Do not "just try it" for a few weeks and tell us afterwards
 * Do not use a client's facilities abroad without client consent - data-residency clauses often forbid access from outside the contracted region
 * Do not rely on a tourist visa to carry out work

TAX CAVEATS
Day-counting differs by jurisdiction (midnight rule vs presence-any-part-of-day). Mobility keeps the master tracker; you remain responsible for correct personal filing. Northwind does not give individual tax advice - consult your own adviser.
